## Purpose

This document is a publicly visible governance-boundary review artifact.

Public accessibility permits inspection, discussion, and feedback. It does
not itself grant authority to treat the artifact as an adopted public claim,
interoperability statement, compatibility statement, endorsement request,
attribution request, or authorization to associate an external participant
or framework with the document.

Its purpose is to examine whether manifest and receipt structures can make
handling expectations more explicit before consequential actions occur.

No endorsement, interoperability determination, compatibility determination,
certification, public attribution, or external association is requested.

# Part I --- Observed Event

This section intentionally separates observation from explanation.

## Observed Sequence

The motivating example is a sequence in which:

1. Material was published.
2. Public association existed.
3. Review and authorization discussions occurred afterward.

The sequence itself is treated as an observable event.

No causal explanation is required for the sequence to exist as an
observation.

## Boundary Question

Can publication, naming, attribution, or public association occur before the
conditions governing those actions have been explicitly established?

This question is independent of why the sequence occurred.

# Part II --- Proposed Handling Model

This section describes the handling approach used within the StegVerse
governance model.

## Design Goal

The objective is not to eliminate disagreement.

The objective is to reduce ambiguity by making handling requirements explicit
before downstream actions occur.

## Core Principle

Consequential actions should not rely solely on implicit expectations.

Handling requirements should be declared, inspectable, and reconstructable.

Visibility and authority are independent state dimensions. Public visibility
must not be interpreted as claim authority, publication authorization,
attribution authorization, endorsement, compatibility, interoperability, or
permission for public association.

## Artifact State Dimensions

A review artifact should independently declare:

- visibility state;
- process or review state;
- claim authority;
- publication authority;
- attribution authority;
- endorsement status;
- interoperability-claim status;
- compatibility-claim status;
- external-association permissions.

Acknowledgement that a reviewer understands these declarations must not be
recorded as agreement, validation, endorsement, acceptance, attribution
permission, or public-association permission.

## Manifest

A manifest may declare:

- claims;
- non-claims;
- visibility state;
- process state;
- claim authority;
- attribution conditions;
- publication conditions;
- external-association conditions;
- delegation conditions;
- downstream handling restrictions;
- expiration conditions;
- review requirements.

## Receipt

A receipt may record:

- what was received;
- when it was received;
- under what handling conditions it was received;
- what actions were permitted;
- what actions were denied;
- what state transitions occurred;
- whether a response acknowledged understanding only;
- whether agreement, validation, endorsement, or acceptance was expressly
  withheld;
- whether attribution or public-association boundaries remained unchanged.

## Example

### Manifest

- public inspection permitted;
- review and feedback permitted;
- adoption as a public claim not permitted;
- naming requires authorization;
- attribution requires authorization;
- public association requires authorization;
- claim inheritance denied.

### Receipt

- draft received;
- process distinction understood;
- no agreement or validation granted;
- no endorsement or acceptance granted;
- no attribution or public-association authority granted;
- no publication authority granted.

### Result

Public accessibility remains admissible for review while unauthorized
publication claims, attribution, endorsement, interoperability claims,
compatibility claims, and external association remain inadmissible.

# Part III --- Claims and Non-Claims

## Claims

This draft claims only that:

1. Explicit handling declarations may reduce ambiguity.
2. Manifest and receipt structures can make handling expectations inspectable.
3. Governance decisions may become more reconstructable when declarations
   accompany inputs.
4. Visibility state and authority state can be represented independently.
5. Acknowledgement of understanding can be represented without converting it
   into agreement, validation, endorsement, or acceptance.

## Non-Claims

This draft does not claim:

1. Perfect prevention of governance failures.
2. Universal applicability.
3. Legal enforceability.
4. Correctness of any implementation.
5. Compatibility with GLM, EVIDE, or any external system.
6. Interoperability with any external framework.
7. Agreement, validation, endorsement, or acceptance by any reviewer.
8. Permission to name, attribute, or publicly associate any reviewer or
   external framework with this artifact.

# Part IV --- Assumptions and Limits

## Assumptions

- Participants can declare handling preferences.
- Manifests can accompany governed inputs.
- Receipts can be retained and reconstructed.
- Independent reviewers can evaluate claims and non-claims.
- Systems can preserve multiple independent state dimensions without
  collapsing visibility into authority.

## Limits

- Explicit declarations cannot eliminate all ambiguity.
- Participants may still disagree on interpretation.
- Missing declarations may require fail-closed behavior.
- Governance mechanisms do not guarantee compliance.
- Public repository hosting may still create social or practical impressions
  of publication even when authority declarations deny that interpretation.
- Terminology alone is insufficient without enforceable transition and receipt
  behavior.

# Part V --- Open Questions

The following questions are intentionally left unresolved.

1. Does the observed sequence represent a sequencing problem, a handling
   problem, an authorization problem, or multiple overlapping concerns?
2. Are there handling classes missing from the manifest model?
3. Are there assumptions that remain implicit?
4. Are the claims or non-claims stated too broadly?
5. Does the proposed handling model appear independently reconstructable?
6. Are there governance-boundary classes that this model fails to capture?
7. What state designation most clearly communicates public visibility without
   implying authorized publication or adoption?
8. What machine-readable receipt form best distinguishes understanding from
   agreement, validation, endorsement, or acceptance?
